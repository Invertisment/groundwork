SAGA / TRANSACTION COMPOSITION

PROPERTY TEST:
- Coordinates two or more independently-owned mutable resources within
  one logical operation, where no single atomic transaction can span
  all of them.

WHY:
- This is real business logic (sequencing, retry-vs-compensate
  decisions), not "just glue." Treating it as boilerplate is where
  sagas actually break in practice — not in the individual steps, but
  in someone assuming the orchestration didn't need the same rigor as
  the core.

MAY NOT EXIST:
- Many operations are single-resource — no saga needed at all.
- Some cross-resource operations are better solved by reconciliation
  (an idempotent periodic sync) than by compensation, when the derived
  state can tolerate brief staleness (e.g. a local cache of an
  externally-owned flag).
- Check "does this touch more than one independently-owned resource,
  and is staleness actually unacceptable" before reaching for saga
  machinery. If either answer is no, this layer collapses away — see
  the single-writer / single-resource case (e.g. a game loop with one
  piece of in-memory state, or the QR scanner app with no DB).

DISTINGUISHING FROM A PLAIN CONTROLLER:
- A saga records durable progress — what step it's on — so a crash
  leaves a trail, not a silent void.
- A controller that just calls two things in sequence, with no durable
  record of intent, is NOT a saga even if it happens to touch two
  resources. It will produce orphaned/half-done state the first time
  the process dies mid-operation, with nothing for a human or an agent
  to diagnose from.

BELONGS HERE:
- Do/compensate pairs per step.
- Retry-vs-rollback decisions (prefer retrying an idempotent pending
  step over rolling back an already-succeeded one, when possible).
- Ordering preconditions between steps.
- Durable intent/progress logging — even a minimal "pending" status
  record is enough to turn silent failure into visible, recoverable
  failure.

TESTING:
- With fakes of the underlying infra — but only fakes that have been
  contract-verified for parity against the real infra (see
  INFRA_LAYER.txt). Never hand-written fakes that were never checked.
- Sequencing/compensation logic gets its own tests (e.g. "does the saga
  correctly compensate when step 2 fails after step 1 succeeded?").
- Property-based/generative testing is a particularly strong fit here —
  arguably stronger than for typical infra. Generate the failure
  point (which step fails), the retry count, and the ordering, then
  assert invariants hold regardless: "no orphaned resource no matter
  which step fails," "replaying a step twice is safe if it's declared
  idempotent," "compensation always leaves the system in a consistent
  state." The failure-mode space here is combinatorial (which step
  fails x in what order x how many retries) and easy to under-enumerate
  by hand as individual examples; a generator covers combinations a
  human wouldn't think to write down. See DECOMPOSITION.txt for why
  this also guards against an implementer special-casing one specific
  failure sequence instead of handling the general case.
